01 / THE RESEARCH

From a new kind of attention
to a new kind of computer.

Modern AI has a growing computational cost. SPIKE explores an alternative: representing and processing information through spikes.

Our collaboration connects algorithms, simulation, and device design. The goal is to build fully spiking transformers and establish a path toward digital and ferroelectric neuromorphic platforms.

A CONCRETE EXAMPLE

What does attention
look like in spikes?

In the SC26 toy demo, four two-bit tokens enter a network. Queries and keys produce binary matches; gates pass matching values to a learned readout. Two output spikes encode the answer.

The example asks the circuit to add the first two token values modulo four while ignoring two distractors.

A neuron integrates.
Then it fires.

A leaky integrate-and-fire neuron accumulates input. When its state exceeds a threshold, it emits a spike and resets. Adjust the input to see the pattern change.

How we describe results and energy

Research objectives and planned hardware validation are goals. The toy attention figures above are reported in the supplied presentation. Its exhaustive verification covers a domain also used for fitting. Energy estimates in the deck depend on operation-cost assumptions and are not measured chip energy. This website makes no measured power or energy-savings claim. HyperNeuro scale targets are design goals, not verified benchmark results.

04 / WHERE WE’RE HEADED

A path from mechanism
to platform.

Project objectives from the SC26 presentation.
These are plans, not completion claims.

PHASE 1 / ALGORITHMS

Develop & test spiking attention

Formulate single-head SAM, implement it in simulation, and evaluate binary tasks and the Cora dataset.

PHASE 1 / DEVICES

Simulate circuit primitives

Develop digital LIF circuits at Duke and ferroelectric neuron–synapse primitives at Delaware.

PHASE 2 / INTEGRATION

Scale learning & validate hardware

Pursue scalable SNN learning, multi-head spiking transformers, chip fabrication, and SAM validation on neuromorphic platforms.
