01 / THE RESEARCH

Connecting spiking AI
with neuromorphic hardware.

As AI models grow, so does the cost of running them. SPIKE investigates how information carried by spikes could support the next generation of scientific AI.

Researchers at ORNL, Duke University, and the University of Delaware connect advances in algorithms, simulation, circuits, and devices. Together, we aim to develop fully spiking transformers and evaluate their path to digital and ferroelectric neuromorphic platforms.

A CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Attention, expressed
through spikes.

Our SC26 demonstration makes the mechanism tangible. Four two-bit tokens enter a network: queries and keys generate binary matches, gates pass matching values, and a learned readout produces two output bits.

The task is deliberately small and fully checkable: add the first two token values modulo four while ignoring two distractors.

A neuron integrates.

04 / WHERE WE’RE HEADED

From foundational ideas
to integrated platforms.

Our research roadmap, from algorithm development
to future hardware validation.

PHASE 1 / ALGORITHMS

Develop & test spiking attention

Formulate single-head SAM, implement it in simulation, and evaluate binary tasks and the Cora dataset.

PHASE 1 / DEVICES

Simulate circuit primitives

Develop digital LIF circuits at Duke and ferroelectric neuron–synapse primitives at Delaware.

PHASE 2 / INTEGRATION

Scale learning & validate hardware

Pursue scalable SNN learning, multi-head spiking transformers, chip fabrication, and SAM validation on neuromorphic platforms.
